# Service — how often will it need a garage?

[← back to overview](README.md) · weak points in [checks.md](checks.md) · compared with the
[La Strada listings](../classic-vans-with-washroom/listings.md)

Written 2026-09-26. **These are estimates** from VW's service plan for the 2.5 TDI, owner forums
(t4forum: many ACV engines run past 400,000 km on the original engine and turbo) and general
experience with vans of this age. Costs are rough independent-garage prices in CZ, not quotes.

**Assumed use:** ≈ 15,000–20,000 km a year (6-month trips with regular driving).

## Short answer

| | Czech Carthago (T4 2.5 TDI, 380,000 km) |
|---|---|
| Planned service | **1 visit a year** (oil etc.), plus **one big visit right after buying** |
| Unplanned repairs | expect **1–2 a year** at this age and mileage — mostly small, sometimes €500–1,500 |
| Money per year | planned ≈ **€300–500**, keep a reserve of ≈ **€1,000–2,000** |
| Engine life | the ACV is in its **late life** at 380,000 km, not at its end — if it was serviced |

## 1. Right after buying (once)

| Job | Why | ≈ Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Both timing belts** + tensioners + **water pump** | if one fails, the valves bend (engine ruined) | €600–1,000 |
| Oil, oil filter, air filter, **fuel filter** | unknown history | €100–150 |
| Brake fluid, coolant check | 2-year fluid | €50–100 |
| Check: clutch, ball joints, bushes, shocks, CV boots, exhaust | common wear at this km | — |
| Leisure battery (and lithium upgrade if doing it) | old lead-acid | see [electrics-upgrade.md](electrics-upgrade.md) |

Skip the belts only if there is **proof** (invoice with km and date) from the last 60,000 km / 5 years.

## 2. Regular plan

| Job | Every | ≈ Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Oil + oil filter** | **10,000–15,000 km or 1 year** | €80–140 |
| Air filter | 30,000 km | €20–40 |
| **Fuel filter** | 30,000 km — **more often after Moroccan / Turkish diesel**; carry a spare | €30–60 |
| Timing belts + water pump | 60,000–80,000 km or 5 years (owners; VW's figure is longer) | €600–1,000 |
| Brake pads / discs | ≈ 40,000–60,000 km | €150–400 |
| Brake fluid | 2 years | €50 |
| **STK** (CZ technical inspection) | 2 years | ≈ €50 + fixes |
| **Gas inspection** (revize) | 2 years (check what the STK needs) | €50–100 |
| Truma heater / boiler, fridge | 2–3 years, or when they fail | €100–200 |
| Roof joint, windows, hatches — look and re-seal | **every year** | €0–200 |

At 15,000–20,000 km a year: **one oil service a year**, belts every **3–4 years**.

## 3. What will probably break (in the next 2–3 years)

At 380,000 km, owners most often report these on the 2.5 TDI and the T4:

| Part | ≈ Cost | How likely |
|---|---|---|
| Clutch (+ flywheel if it is dual-mass) | €600–1,200 | high, unless done recently — ask |
| Front ball joints, rear arm bushes, shocks | €300–800 | high |
| **VP37 injection pump** seals / quantity adjuster | €500–1,500 | medium |
| Injectors | €400–800 | medium |
| Turbo, vacuum lines, N75 valve | €100–1,000 | medium |
| Rust welding before an STK | €300–1,500 | medium — depends on what you see at the viewing |
| Alternator, starter, glow plugs | €100–400 each | medium |
| Cylinder head crack (cylinder 3) | €1,000+ | low, but the big one |

**Good news:** T4 parts are everywhere, cheap, and every garage knows this engine.

## 4. Against the La Strada listings

| | **Czech Carthago** | **La Strada 312D** (Waltershausen / Ebermannsdorf) | **La Strada V6 212D** (Berlin) | **La Strada Jumper 2.8 HDi** (Hamburg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | ≈ €14,700 | €18,500 / €14,700 | €16,500 VB | €19,999 |
| Year / km | 1997 / **380,000** | 1999 / 212,000 · 1997 / 207,000 | 1997 / 294,000 | 2005 / 236,500 |
| Engine | VW 2.5 TDI, 102 PS | **Mercedes OM602 2.9, 122 PS** | same OM602 | Iveco 2.8 HDi, 128 PS (common rail) |
| Engine life at that km | **late** | **mid-life** — OM602 often runs 500,000–800,000 km | mid-to-late | mid-life |
| **Timing** | **2 belts** — change every 60–80,000 km | **chain** — no belt changes; check the chain at ≈ 300,000 | chain | belt (≈ 100–120,000 km, check) — **done 02/2026** |
| Injection | electronic VP37 pump | **mechanical pump** — simple, tolerant of bad diesel | mechanical | common rail — more electronics, less tolerant of bad fuel |
| Engine garage visits | **more** | **fewest** | few | medium |
| **Rust risk** | T4: medium | **Sprinter T1N: high** — the main cost on these | high | Jumper: medium |
| Body garage visits | medium | **more** (welding before inspections is common) | more | medium |
| Upgrades already done | none | — | **lithium, solar, compressor fridge** | €6,200 of work 2026 (belt, clutch, injectors, rust treatment) |
| Size | **5.1 m**, 1.84 wide | ≈ 5.6 m, 1.93 wide | ≈ 5.6 m | ≈ 5.6 m, 2.0 wide |
| Diesel | 10–12 L | 11–13 L | 11–13 L | 10–12 L |
| Buying | CZ papers, dealer, free revize | German import (export plates, CZ registration) | import | import |

**Reading it:**

- **Engine:** the La Strada Sprinters win clearly. The OM602 has **no belts**, a mechanical pump
  and is at **half the km**. Fewer engine visits, and it copes with bad diesel in Morocco.
- **Body:** the Sprinter T1N rusts more than the T4. Expect welding at inspections. **The rust you
  see at the viewing decides the real cost.**
- **Total:** a good La Strada 312D at ≈ 210,000 km should need **fewer garage visits overall**
  than the Czech Carthago at 380,000 km — **if its body is sound.** It costs €0–4k more and needs
  an import.
- **Best-prepared:** the **Berlin V6** already has the electrics we would add (≈ €1.5k saved); the
  **Hamburg Jumper** has just had €6,200 of work.
- **Czech Carthago's edge:** smaller (easier in towns and narrow roads), Czech papers, table
  seating we know works, a dealer who shows everything working.
