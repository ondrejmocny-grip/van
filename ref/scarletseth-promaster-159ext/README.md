# Reference: Scarlet & Seth — Ram ProMaster 159" EXT, high roof

**Status:** analysed, layout reconstructed. A big, expensive van — useful for **two ideas**,
not as a whole layout.

## Layout

See `layout.png` / `layout.svg`. Front at left, driver side at the bottom.

Front to rear:

1. **Closed cab.** A full insulated divider with a **pocket door**. **No swivel seats** —
   they say the ProMaster seats sit comically high and they would not have used the space.
2. **Shower across the front, parallel to the cab divider.** This is the idea worth having:
   turning the shower sideways and parking it against the bulkhead frees the entire middle
   of the van. **The entrance is on its short side**, a narrow opening, deliberately so that
   nobody looks into it from the kitchen or the living space. Their stated minimum internal
   size was **26 × 40 in (660 × 1016 mm)**.
   - Finish: **Portola "Piano Room" Roman clay over Schluter Kerdi board**, 14 coats, ~3 weeks,
     and only **5–7 lb** of added weight — chosen over tile purely for weight.
   - A **boat-hatch skylight** directly over it, and a teak slatted floor.
   - Door: **Stoett Nautilus** roll-type waterproof shower door.
   - They ran **4 years across two vans with no fixed shower** before this one, and their
     advice is that a fixed shower is only worth it in a long-wheelbase high-roof van.
3. **Toilet in a drawer under the fridge.** A **Compo Closet Cuddy** (16.3 in tall — that
   height is the whole trick) on **250 lb locking slides**, behind a 10×10 in stainless access
   door, that **pulls out into the shower**. This is the second idea worth having: the WC
   costs no floor area at all.
4. **Passenger side: sliding door, floor deliberately kept clear** for them and the cats.
5. **Galley, split across the aisle:**
   - driver side — fridge (with the toilet drawer under it), then sink, prep space, an oven,
     drawer stacks, pull-out bins, open shelves and overhead lockers.
   - passenger side, immediately aft of the sliding door — a second counter with storage and
     **a gas hob that slides forward out of the counter's end**, deploying into the doorway.
     Stow it and the worktop is flat and the aisle is clear. Visible at 0:07–0:08 in the short.
   - **A quarter-round unit in the corner where the galley run meets the shower wall** —
     trash and recycling inside, a flat top you can perch on. It rounds off the one internal
     corner you would otherwise catch a hip on every time you pass, and it turns dead corner
     volume into the bin. Epoxy worktops, fluted red-oak veneer fronts, rounded counter ends
     throughout — there is not a square corner at hip height anywhere in the van.
6. **Rear: a U-shaped dinette.** Benches both sides, **starting at the same line on both
   sides**, just aft of the two galley counters. Drop the table and it becomes a
   **near-square ~1890 × 1870 mm platform**, so you can sleep along the van or across it.
   A litter box lives in the forward end of the passenger bench.

## Systems

- **Power:** **810 Ah at 12 V** (Battle Born GC3 heated LiFePO₄), Victron incl. Cerbo GX,
  3000 W inverter. Sized for heated floors, an oven and air conditioning.
- **Solar:** **600 W semi-rigid walk-on marine panels**, 6 mm thick, **stuck down with
  C-channel and VHB tape — no roof rack, no holes**. They regularly see 550 W+ from 600 W.
  Their ranking after 12 builds: semi-rigid > rigid > semi-flexible, and they say never buy
  semi-flexible (theirs fell to 45–55% in 3 months).
- **Water:** 48 gal fresh + 30 gal grey in **6 underslung tanks**, heat pads, heat trace,
  insulated, skid plates. The ProMaster exhaust had to be rerouted for them. No tanks inside
  — a deliberate consequence of the open-plan floor.
  They shower on **under 1 gallon**.
- **Air con:** Cruise N Comfort HD 12L 12 V mini-split, condenser under the van. **14–16 hours
  continuous** off the 810 Ah bank. In hindsight they recommend a **Dometic RTX 2000** instead.
- **Heat:** diesel heater plus **heated tile floors**.

## What to steal / what to question

Worth stealing:
- **Shower turned sideways against the cab bulkhead.** On our L3H3 the wet cubicle currently
  eats 750 mm of the driver-side run at x 1600–2350. **Taken up in [v2](../../v2/README.md)**,
  in the half-width form — front driver corner, which frees the middle of the van.
- **Toilet in a drawer under the fridge, pulling into the shower.** Our cubicle already
  doubles as the WC, but a drawer would give the cubicle its floor back while showering.
  The Cuddy's 16.3 in height is what makes it possible; check it against our fridge plinth.
- **Roman clay instead of tile.** 5–7 lb for a whole shower is a real weight argument, and
  we are not tiling a Moroccan road trip if we can avoid it.
- **Walk-on semi-rigid solar stuck down with VHB, no rack.** Saves the roof rack entirely,
  and their "never buy semi-flexible" data point is worth more than the rest of this file.
- **Under 1 gallon per shower.** Same discipline we already assumed. Nice to see it measured.
- **Rounding off every corner at hip height**, and making the worst one a bin you can sit on.
  Our corridor past the cubicle is 532 mm — the tightest spot in v1, and
  exactly where a quarter-round would earn its keep.
- **A hob that slides out of the side of the counter.** It buys back the whole worktop when
  you are not cooking, and it costs no floor area — worth weighing against v1's fixed
  induction top, which permanently owns its patch of the galley run.

Worth questioning for us:
- **Everything underslung.** 6 tanks under a van heading for Moroccan pistes, with rerouted
  exhaust and skid plates, is a lot of exposure. Our 110 L inside the bench is duller and safer.
- **No swivel seats.** Their reasoning is ProMaster-specific (seat height) and they had no
  office to build. It did not transfer while v1's front lounge was our main living space — but
  **v2 drops the swivels anyway**, for a different reason: a 3-seat cab bench leaves nothing
  to swivel.
- **A 4089 mm van.** 639 mm longer than ours. Read the layout for ideas, not for proportions.
- **Air conditioning.** 14–16 h on 810 Ah. At our battery size it is not a conversation.
  Chasing warm-but-not-hot weather stays the plan.
- **No desk.** Same gap as every reference so far.
