## Layout

See `layout.png` / `layout.svg`. Front at left, driver side at the bottom.

Front to rear:

1. **Front (full width): desk / office.** Counter against the cab bulkhead, 32" monitor on a
   swivel arm, 3 deep drawers + 2 small pull-outs, charging station (120 V strip + 2× 12 V),
   upper cabinets plus a shelf over the cab, and a pass-through to the cab underneath.
2. **Driver side: wet bath**, directly opposite the sliding door. Arched opening with a curtain,
   fully tiled (5×5 Moroccan tile), rainfall head + detachable sprayer, recessed niche for
   bottles, puck light, Laveo dry-flush toilet (they did not want composting).
3. **Passenger side: the sliding door is the entry**, ~1300 mm of open floor. Bug screen fitted.
4. **Driver side: fridge**, Isotherm Cruise 130 (12 V), door facing aft down the aisle.
   Cabinet above (dog food, toiletries), cabinets below (shoes, 4 gal Bosch water heater).
5. **Galley, split across the aisle** (~540 mm aisle):
   - driver side — large white farmhouse sink, gold bridge tap with hot + cold, separate
     filtered-water spout, bar-style cup rinser. Under it: water-pump switch, cleaning
     supplies, vented pull-out bins, small bin.
   - passenger side — 3-burner propane hob + oven combo (sealed propane locker below,
     solenoid valve switch), then 3 large pull-out drawers.
   - both sides — an **arched bump-out niche** recessed into the wall above the counter
     (mugs, glasses, chopping boards, hidden knife block), spice rack, big overhead cabinets.
6. **Rear: elevated dinette → bed.** Benches down both sides, poplar table on a **Lagun swivel
   mount** (rotates for eating, cards, or facing the monitor). Table drops to make an
   "almost queen" bed, roughly 1870 × 1780 mm. Storage inside both benches, 2 pull-out
   drawers, 2 more big drawers under the dinette step, and a pull-out pantry at the step.
7. **Garage under the dinette**, reached through the rear doors: overhead "snowboard shelf"
   (snowboards, camp chairs, awning), 2 deep tool drawers, electrical cabinet.

## Systems

- **Power:** 620 W solar on a Unaka Gear Co 148 HD roof rack (mixed panel sizes to fit around
  the fans) · 4 × 100 Ah Dakota Lithium · Victron MultiPlus 3000 W inverter/charger ·
  Victron Orion 12/12-50 DC-DC · Victron MPPT · Lynx distributor · Cerbo GX touchscreen ·
  shore power inlet. Three charging paths: solar, alternator, shore.
- **Water:** 35 gal (~132 L) fresh, in an **over-the-wheel-well tank** (Northwest Conversions) —
  she calls this the single biggest storage win. 4 gal Bosch on-demand hot water on a
  switched outlet with a 15-minute timer. Outdoor shower.
- **Heat / air:** diesel heater + auxiliary diesel tank · 2 × Maxxair fans (front + over the
  bed, deliberately a wind tunnel) · 12 V Sirocco fan at the rear.
- **Connectivity:** Starlink cut down and **flat-mounted** (Star Mount) — works while driving,
  comes on with the inverter.
- **Windows:** Arctic Tern awning windows with integral fly screens and blinds.
- **Lighting:** 12 V LED strips, one zone per area, dimmable at the rear.

## What to steal / what to question

Worth stealing:
- Wet bath opposite the sliding door — you get the whole doorway as changing space.
- Split galley: the 540 mm aisle doubles as the work triangle, and both walls earn their keep.
- Over-the-wheel-well water tank instead of under the bed.
- Arched niches: real storage out of dead wall depth, and the thing that makes it look built.
- Lagun table: one surface serves dinner, cards, desk 2, and the bed base.

Worth questioning for us — **all now answered in [v1](../../v1/README.md)**:
- Propane oven costs a sealed locker and a big chunk of the passenger-side run. Do we bake?
- The rear bed is ~1.87 m long. Fine for them, needs checking against our height.
- Full-height wet bath eats ~0.9 m of a 4.37 m van. Alternatives: outdoor shower only, or a
  shower tray under a lift-out floor.
- Their van is US LHD with the slider on the kerb (right) side. Czechia also drives on the
  right, so an EU van has the slider on the right too — the layout carries over unmirrored.
  Many EU panel vans offer a second slider on the left; decide if we want one, it costs a wall.
- No fixed bed = the bed gets made and unmade every day. Two years of that is a real vote
  of confidence, but it is still a daily chore.
